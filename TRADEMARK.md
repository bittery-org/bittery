# The Bittery Name

**Short version: the code is free, the name is ours. If you ship a modified version, please give it your own name.**

Bittery™ is free software under the AGPLv3 and GPLv3. Those licenses give you real rights — use it, change it, share it, run it for anyone, even compete with us. We mean that, and nothing here takes any of it back.

This page is only about the *name*.

## Please rename your fork

If you distribute a modified version of Bittery, pick a different name and drop our logo. Something clearly yours, not `Bittery-NG` or `OpenBittery`.

It's not that we're precious about it. Bittery is a password manager, and people install it because they trust whoever built it got the crypto right. If any build could carry our name, that trust would stop meaning anything — and the people least able to check a binary are exactly the ones who'd get hurt. Your fork deserves its own name anyway.

## Things that are completely fine

- Saying your project is a fork of Bittery, based on Bittery, or compatible with Bittery
- Writing about us, reviewing us, comparing us to others, criticising us
- Linking to us, talking about us, teaching with our code
- Packaging or self-hosting our **unmodified** releases under the name Bittery

You don't need permission for any of that. Just don't imply we endorse or maintain your version.

## Where we actually stand

We have **no registered trademark**. "Bittery" is an unregistered mark — we use it in commerce, and that gives us common-law rights in some places and not much anywhere else. We'd like to register it when we can afford to; we can't yet, so we're not going to pretend otherwise. You'll only ever see ™ from us, never ®.

We're writing this down mainly so our intent is on the record and nobody is surprised later.

## If we ever have a problem

We'd email you. That's it.

We only care about people passing off modified software as ours, or implying we endorse something we don't. We have no interest in using the name to lean on forks, competitors, packagers, or anyone criticising us — and if you think you're playing fair and we get in touch anyway, just say so. It's far more likely we misread something than that you did anything wrong.

Questions, or want to use the name in a way that isn't covered here? `legal@bittery.com` — we're friendly.
